About Me

I am an incoming Master of Data Science student at The University of Hong Kong (HKU), School of Computing and Data Science, starting Fall 2026.

My research interests lie in Multimodal Learning, Reinforcement Learning, and Embodied AI. I want to build models that act on the world, not just describe it, and reinforcement learning is how they learn to. Training RL agents at Li Auto (理想汽车) convinced me that the real bottleneck usually isn't compute but verifiable reward: RL only becomes genuine capability when the reward signal is trustworthy. That is the thread I want to keep pulling, from computer-use agents, where digital environments give scalable and checkable reward, all the way to physical-world grounding, where the same problem gets much harder and much more worth solving.

At Li Auto's Foundation Model team, I worked on the post-training of a 35B (3B-activated) MoE model, owning the reinforcement learning for two domains (instruction-following and code generation) and building the reward infrastructure underneath them (sandbox code-execution rewards, reward-hacking detection). More recently our team has moved into embodied intelligence, and I joined the perception and OCR data track, where I am currently helping build the data and training infrastructure. Before Li Auto, I completed my B.E. at Xidian University with an Outstanding Thesis distinction (24/580), working on multimodal (RGB-T) tracking and physical-domain adversarial robustness.

🎯 I am actively seeking research internships in Reinforcement Learning for agents, Multimodal Learning, and Embodied AI, available immediately. Longer term, I aim to pursue a PhD in these directions. I'd love the opportunity to collaborate!

Overview: My Contributions to Mach-Mind-4-Flash Post-Training
Mach-Mind-4-Flash was a team effort; within it I owned the reinforcement learning for two domains, instruction-following and code generation. My focus was a question I kept coming back to: how to make RL turn compute into genuine capability instead of reward exploitation. On the code side I built a sandbox-based execution reward system for verifiable signal; on the instruction-following side I diagnosed reward hacking through trajectory-level analysis and replaced the rule-based reward with an LLM-as-judge verification pipeline, which cut the reward-hacking rate by 34.4% relative and held it below the base model's own level for the rest of training. I also explored training-free MoE merging as a complementary capability-fusion path. These pieces fed into the team's broader model, which my teammates carried into the downstream MoPD on-device planning agent. More recently our team moved into embodied intelligence, and I joined its perception and OCR data track, currently building out the data and training infrastructure.
Domain RL for Instruction-Following & Code Generation (Mach-Mind-4-Flash)
Within the team's post-training pipeline for a 35B (3B-activated) MoE model, I was responsible for the reinforcement learning of two domains: instruction-following and code generation. Starting from a post-SFT checkpoint, I ran domain RL with systematic data-composition ablations to find stable training schedules, and studied how sequencing and data mix affect capability retention. My contributions fed into the broader model reported in the Mach-Mind-4-Flash technical report, which reached performance on par with, and on several tasks surpassing, 100B-parameter-class models through post-training alone.
Reward Hacking Detection & Mitigation for RL Training
Identified reward hacking through trajectory-level case analysis during instruction-following RL training, where a rule-based reward was being gamed by format-level exploits. Designed an LLM-as-judge verification mechanism and automated audit pipeline to separate genuine capability gains from format-gaming. Replacing the rule-based reward with this pipeline reduced the reward-hacking rate by 34.4% relative, and past the early training phase it stabilized below the base model's own hacking level, giving reliable signal for reward-shaping and early-stopping decisions.
Code RL Reward System — Sandbox-Based Execution Pipeline
Reproduced and adapted the XCoder code reward system for internal Code RL training. Implemented a full sandbox-based execution reward pipeline: AST compilation check → dual-verification test generation → batch sandboxed evaluation, combined with format reward scoring. Supports GRPO-based Code RL on large MoE models with configurable multi-dimensional reward composition.
SWE Code Agent — Large-Scale Training Data Construction
Independently led large-scale training data construction for SWE code agents. Surveyed 10+ open-source Code Agent datasets and performed cross-dataset comparative analysis on quality distribution, model behavioral tendencies, and problem-solving efficiency. Selected and integrated 6 optimal benchmarks covering 30K+ Issues and 100K+ Trajectories. Designed an automated quality filtering pipeline combining rule engines with LLM-based joint review, systematically eliminating low-quality and reward-hacking samples to produce high-confidence training data delivered to downstream teams for production use.
Training-Free MoE Model Merging (Complementary Path to RL)
As a complementary approach to RL training, explored training-free merging for 35B-A3B MoE models. Proposed cosine-alignment + Hungarian optimal matching for expert pairing, with independent retention and interpolation fusion for Attention layers. Validated end-to-end on IFEval, LiveCodeBench, AIME 2026. Conducted systematic comparison with the RL path to quantify capability gains and forgetting across both approaches, informing the team's final multi-capability fusion strategy.
MoPD: Multimodal On-Device Planning — SFT Data Production

Xidian University · Undergraduate Thesis

Research Assistant · Nov 2025 – May 2026 · Thesis rated "Outstanding" (highest level)
Modality Quality Reliability Calibration for Multi-Modal Tracking (MQRC)
Diagnosed systematic overconfidence in FMTrack multi-expert fusion reliability prediction, caused by end-to-end training coupling with localization loss. Proposed MQRC, a lightweight module with only 284 trainable parameters that performs per-expert temperature scaling and residual MLP projection on reshaped 32-dim logits, injected via forward hooks into the frozen 286M-parameter model. Achieved +2.5% MPR / +1.7% MSR on RGBT234, +0.3% SR on LasHeR, +0.9% MSR on RGBT210.
Synthetic Degradation Contrastive Alignment for Calibration
Addressed the fundamental challenge that per-frame modality quality annotations are unavailable. Designed a synthetic degradation contrastive alignment mechanism decoupled from the tracking objective. Constructed 7 programmatic degradation types (RGB: random occlusion, Gaussian noise, low-light compression, motion blur; TIR: contrast compression, stripe noise, pixel dropout) to generate quality-ordered triplets, optimized with margin ranking loss and modality-independent gradient paths.

🏅 Selected Competition Papers

Modeling Momentum Dynamics in Tennis: A Data-Driven Approach Using Weighted LSTM and Markov Chain Analysis
MCM/ICM 2024 · Team #2429459 · Meritorious Winner (一等奖)
Developed a comprehensive framework for quantifying "momentum" in competitive tennis. Key technical contributions:
① Player Momentum Capture Model: Defined composite momentum indicators from real-time match statistics (serve speed, break points, unforced errors) and visualized momentum-score dynamics across sets.
② Weighted LSTM Network: Designed a temporal neural network with attention-weighted inputs to predict point-level momentum state transitions, achieving 95.02% prediction accuracy on Wimbledon 2023 data.
③ Markov Chain Volatility Model: Built a stochastic model capturing momentum shifts as state transitions, combined with multivariate nonlinear regression for fluctuation prediction. Validated statistical significance via Monte Carlo simulation.
④ Generalization: Extended the framework to badminton and table tennis, achieving >85% prediction accuracy across different sports, confirming the model's domain-transfer capability.

Optimal Crop Allocation via Linear Programming under Multi-Constraint Agricultural Planning
CUMCM 2023 · First Prize (赛区一等奖)
Developed a linear programming-based crop allocation model maximizing total net profit for farmland under multiple real-world constraints. Preprocessed 41 crop types into 7 categories by planting conditions, then formulated an optimization model incorporating area limits, demand thresholds, and co-planting constraints. Introduced stochastic and environmental variables to represent real-world uncertainty, applying adaptive gradient algorithms to boost expected profit to 129.23% of the 2023 baseline. Further optimized inter-crop relationships via ridge regression, hierarchical clustering, and entropy-weighted TOPSIS, improving profit by ~5%. Validated model robustness through sensitivity analysis and simulated annealing, confirming practical applicability for agricultural planning.
